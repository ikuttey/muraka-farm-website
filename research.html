Research · Monitoring · Documentation

Learning from every reef we restore.

Coral restoration is not only about placing corals back on the reef. Long-term restoration requires observation, documentation and learning from how corals respond over time.

Muraka Farm uses monitoring and field observations to understand restoration progress, improve our methods and build a stronger evidence base for community-led reef restoration in the Maldives.

Monitoring practice

How we monitor restoration

Monitoring helps us understand what is working, where challenges appear and how restoration methods can improve over time.

01

Survival

Track whether restored coral fragments remain alive following nursery and restoration activities.

02

Growth

Document how coral fragments and colonies develop over time.

03

Coral health

Observe visible signs of stress, damage, disease, predation and general coral condition.

04

Bleaching

Record signs of bleaching and thermal stress during monitoring activities.

05

Restoration structures

Observe the condition and stability of Muraka frames and other restoration structures.

06

Photo documentation

Use repeat photographs to create a visual record of restoration progress and changing reef conditions.

Monitoring journey

From nursery care to long-term learning.

Restoration continues well beyond the moment coral is returned to a damaged reef area.

  1. Nursery
  2. Transfer to Muraka frame
  3. Restoration site
  4. Monitor
  5. Document
  6. Learn & adapt

Restoration is an ongoing process. Monitoring continues after coral fragments leave the nursery and are transferred onto Muraka frames in damaged reef areas.

The information collected through monitoring helps guide future restoration decisions and long-term reef stewardship.

Field research in Dharavandhoo

A living reef laboratory.

Based in Dharavandhoo in Baa Atoll, Muraka Farm works directly within the reef environment where restoration, local livelihoods and community stewardship intersect.

Our restoration sites and community-based work can provide opportunities for researchers and students interested in coral restoration, reef ecology, environmental education and locally led conservation.

From action to evidence

Documenting progress and challenges.

Every monitoring session adds to our understanding of how restored corals respond over time. By documenting both progress and challenges, Muraka Farm aims to strengthen restoration practice while contributing to a more transparent and locally grounded approach to reef conservation.
